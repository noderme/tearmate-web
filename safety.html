Safety & Crisis Policy

Last updated: April 12, 2026

Our commitment

TearMate exists to connect people seeking emotional support with compassionate listeners. We take the safety of every person on our platform seriously. This page explains exactly what we allow, what we do not allow, and what happens if someone on TearMate appears to be in danger.

What is and is not permitted

TearMate does not permit:

  • Content that encourages, glorifies, or instructs self-harm or suicide.
  • Sharing methods, means, or locations associated with self-harm or suicide.
  • Content that pressures another user toward self-harm.

TearMate does permit:

  • Openly sharing that you are struggling, in pain, or having dark thoughts — this is why the app exists.
  • Helpers offering compassionate, non-judgmental listening.
  • Discussion of mental health experiences in a supportive context.

TearMate is not a crisis service and is not a substitute for professional mental health care.

How we detect and respond to crisis signals

TearMate operates a two-layer automated safety system:

  1. Client-side keyword screening: certain phrases associated with immediate danger are detected the moment a message is composed. Before the message is sent, the user is shown emergency helpline numbers and must acknowledge them.
  2. AI-powered message analysis: every message sent through the platform is analysed by an automated system to detect crisis signals that keywords alone may miss — including context, tone, and phrasing. When a crisis signal is detected, both users in the session are immediately shown emergency helpline numbers.

Neither layer blocks users from continuing to use the app. Our role is to ensure that everyone who may be in danger knows where to get immediate help.

Emergency helplines

When a crisis signal is detected, users are shown:

988 — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, United States (call or text, free, 24/7)

116 123 — Samaritans, United Kingdom (free, 24/7)

999 / 911 — Emergency services (UK / US)

These are displayed as tappable links that dial directly from within the app.

Reporting harmful content

Every user profile has a Report button. If you encounter content or behaviour that encourages self-harm or endangers another user, please report it immediately. We review all reports and take action within 24 hours. In cases of imminent risk, we may contact emergency services if a user has provided identifiable location information.

To report a concern, use the in-app Report button on the relevant profile, or contact us directly at safety@tearmate.online.

What TearMate is not

TearMate is not a crisis line, a mental health service, a therapy service, or an emergency service. Helpers on TearMate are not licensed therapists or medical professionals. If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, please call emergency services (999 in the UK, 911 in the US) or a crisis helpline immediately.

Compliance

This policy is maintained in accordance with the UK Online Safety Act 2023, Ofcom guidance on suicide and self-harm content, and US best practices for platforms providing emotionally sensitive services. Our automated safety systems and this policy are reviewed regularly.

Contact

For safety concerns, policy questions, or to report a welfare issue, contact us at safety@tearmate.online.
